Traficom: Cybermeter - User guide (https://terokarvinen.com/kybermittari/files/Kybermittari_Cybermeter_User_Guide_V1.pdf):
  Summary and checklist of the assessment process (p 10)
  
  - A five-step continuous cycle of Cybermeter assessment is condensed on the page 10. These five steps are:
  - 1. Initiate assessment
       -This step consists mainly of management responsibilities
    2. Prepare for assessment
       -This step consists mainly of assessment sponsor and facilitator responsibilities
    3. Conduct assessment
       -This step consists mainly of facilitator and participants responsibilities
    4. Identify development activities
       -This step consists mainly of facilitator, sponsor, specialists and development owners responsibilities
    5. Perform activities and update assessment
        -This step consists mainly of development-plan owners, specialists and management responsibilities
  - Assessment is not the end result, that is the key takeaway!
  - According to NCSC-FI & Traficom, the reassessment should be done every one to two years. Depending on the assessed operating area and organisation.

  3 Cybermeter maturity model (p 11-14)
  
  - Consisting of four maturity levels from 0 to 3, it evaluates organization's cybersecurity management and how systematically it is being done.
  - 0 is missing, 1 is basic, 2 is managed and 3 is measured & improved continuously.
  - Cybersecurity is being examined through 11 domains by cybermeter.
  - Across the 11 domains there are are 52 objectives inside the Cybermeter.
  - Under these objectives, there are individual practices that are assessed with four different responses.
  - Maturity level is also being calculated in three different stages: 1. Assess practices and stages 2 & 3 that are related to objective maturity.

  The Cybermeter maturity report (p 23)
  
  - An detailed technical report produced by the self-assessment tool of the Cybermeter, by presenting the results of 11 cybersecurity domains.
  - Basically an cybersecurity gap-analysis dashboard. Not only a scorecard.
  - Used to reveal the situation of organization and where it wants to be. Giving the information to close the gap with the needed development.

- There are separate assessment sheets for the 11 Cybersecurity domains in Cybermeter with detailed objectives and practices under each domain.
- For each practice there are four different response options with free-text fields for evidence, references and comments. 
- Provides three reporting perspectives: Cybermeter maturity report, Detailed NIST CSF Core report and Management maturity report.

b) Sucks or succeeds. How could Kybermittari process be really useless? How could it be really useful? (No tests on computer required.)

- In my mind, Kybermittari could potentially suck in a couple of cases like:
  - If one person does the assessment alone. Because several different aspects and experts are needed to agree on the assessments.
  - If the organization does not challenge the answers and tries to be generous to the scoring of their processes.
  - No actions are taken after the assessment is done. 
  - Instead of making decisions, the scoring of the processes becomes more important for the management.
  - If the whole process falls in the hands of cybersecurity, without the help from other departments.
 
- From my perspective, Kybermittari succeeds in cases like these:
  - When the maturity rating is based on evidence and not on opinions.
  - The findings are actually turned into actions, making the kybermittari an improvement process.
  - When the organization does not try to fix everything at once, but prioritizes for an example on the business impact.
  - When there are several experts from different parts inside the organization. This brings more value to the conversations and overall scoring.
  - When disagreements become agreements. Thus making discoveries for the organization to use in improvement.

c) Competition. What approaches, models or tools could compete with Kybermittari? 
Are there any benefits or downsides for those? Feel free to find your own sources. (No tests on computer required. 1-2 examples are enough.)

- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: This can be used for organization-wide program in cybersecurity.
  + Is easy to use for connecting with business objectives. Helps with the support of gap analysis.
  - This is non-prescriptive, so the organization must define different aspects for achieving desired outcomes.

- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1: Better than Kybermittari, if the organization needs a prioritized technical improvement backlog.
  + Safeguards are designed to be measurable and practical,
  - Not an complete organizational maturity model, primary a control implementation model.
