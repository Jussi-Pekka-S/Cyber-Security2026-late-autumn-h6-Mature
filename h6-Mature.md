Traficom: Cybermeter - User guide (https://terokarvinen.com/kybermittari/files/Kybermittari_Cybermeter_User_Guide_V1.pdf):
  Summary and checklist of the assessment process (p 10)
  
  - A five-step continuous cycle of Cybermeter assessment is condensed on the page 10. These five steps are:
  - 1. Initiate assessment
       -This step consists mainly of management responsibilities
    2. Prepare for assessment
       -This step consists mainly of assessment sponsor and facilitator responsibilities
    3. Conduct assessment
       -This step consists mainly of facilitator and participants responsibilities
    4. Identify development activities
       -This step consists mainly of facilitator, sponsor, specialists and development owners responsibilities
    5. Perform activities and update assessment
        -This step consists mainly of development-plan owners, specialists and management responsibilities
  - Assessment is not the end result, that is the key takeaway!
  - According to NCSC-FI & Traficom, the reassessment should be done every one to two years. Depending on the assessed operating area and organisation.

  3 Cybermeter maturity model (p 11-14)
  
  - Consisting of four maturity levels from 0 to 3, it evaluates organization's cybersecurity management and how systematically it is being done.
  - 0 is missing, 1 is basic, 2 is managed and 3 is measured & improved continuously.
  - Cybersecurity is being examined through 11 domains by cybermeter.
  - Across the 11 domains there are are 52 objectives inside the Cybermeter.
  - Under these objectives, there are individual practices that are assessed with four different responses.
  - Maturity level is also being calculated in three different stages: 1. Assess practices and stages 2 & 3 that are related to objective maturity.

  The Cybermeter maturity report (p 23)
  
  - An detailed technical report produced by the self-assessment tool of the Cybermeter, by presenting the results of 11 cybersecurity domains.
  - Basically an cybersecurity gap-analysis dashboard. Not only a scorecard.
  - Used to reveal the situation of organization and where it wants to be. Giving the information to close the gap with the needed development.
